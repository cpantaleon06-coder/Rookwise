# SYNTHETIC runbook written for a Rookwise smoke test. Fictional service "parcelpost-api".
Runbook: parcelpost-api returns HTTP 503
1. Confirm the alert ParcelpostHigh5xx and the error type in the service log.
2. If the errors are PoolTimeout, the service is waiting for a database connection.
3. Compare db.pool active with max: active equal to max with a growing queue means the pool is saturated.
4. Check the database side (pg-primary.internal connections and cpu_pct) before blaming the database.
5. A healthy database with a saturated pool points to the pool size, not the database.
6. Look for a config.diff or deploy.agent line shortly before the first PoolTimeout.
7. db.pool.max_connections must stay at 50 or more in production (capacity plan CP-12).
8. To mitigate, roll back to the last good config_version with deploy.agent rollback.
9. Do not flush the Redis cache to fix 503s: it raises the miss rate and the database load.
10. Cache miss rate warnings above 0.20 are informational unless above 0.50.
11. GC pauses under 500 ms are normal for this service.
12. Disk warnings under 90 percent are not urgent.
13. After mitigation, confirm healthcheck ok and the alert resolved.
14. Record the change ticket of the bad config in the incident report.
15. Open a follow-up to add a validation rule for db.pool.max_connections in CI.
Escalation: page the platform team if a rollback does not clear the errors within 10 minutes.
Owner: platform team (fictional). Last reviewed: 2026-02-10 (fictional).
End of runbook.
